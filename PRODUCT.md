# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Curious people encountering a population cartogram for the first time, opening it on a laptop or phone. They should understand the transformation through watching and touching it, without knowing optimal transport or reading a mathematical introduction.

## Product Purpose

Make one beautiful, reversible 2D transformation in which the same identifiable units of humanity carry familiar geography into human space, so a viewer immediately feels why densely inhabited places are big. Preserve enough geography to stay oriented, then use colour to reveal differences across that human world.

Phil's original feeling: places with many people feel full and big; sparsely inhabited places can be geographically vast but feel empty and small. The experience must make that observation visible.

## Brand Personality

Tactile, lucid, inquisitive. A living atlas with the clarity of a museum exhibit. The map is the spectacle; the surrounding interface is quiet and exact.

## Anti-references

An experiment gallery with disconnected maps; an algorithm dashboard; unreadable labels; bloated or melted shapes with no geographical bearings; a beautiful effect whose population claim is false.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve the same population units and geographical anchors throughout the journey. Reversing must return to the same world.
2. Make one action explain the idea: familiar geography becomes human space.
3. Prefer geographical recognition to an unqualified claim of mathematical purity. State the compromise plainly.
4. Colour answers a second question on the same world. It must not silently change the meaning of area.
5. Judge the live experience, not the number of implemented features or the quality of isolated renders.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, a paused initial state, reduced-motion support, readable labels, touch interaction and a text account of what the map means. Colour scales must have written meanings and values; a selected unit must remain identifiable without colour alone.
