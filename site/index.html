What changes, and what stays the same +

One million, everywhere.

Each cell represents approximately one million people in the 2025 population model. The same cells survive every step of the animation. Select one, then move the slider back and forth.

These are statistical groups spread over a patch of land. Dots identify these same groups throughout the change of scale. Some cells cross national borders.

A little geography stays.

The map opens up densely inhabited places and compresses sparsely inhabited ones. Ocean space, smoothing and a small geographical component keep the world recognisable.

The cells approach a more even fabric, but their displayed areas and shapes are not perfectly equal. “One million” describes their population, not an exact square on the screen.

Colour asks another question.

Area always follows the same 2025 population. Output per person uses economic output and population from 2015. The history lens compares the share of 2015 output with the share of accumulated person-years from 10,000 BC to 2023.

These are spatial comparisons. They do not establish why places became wealthy. GDP measures economic output, not personal income or accumulated wealth.

Data & method. GHS-POP R2023A (2025 projection and 2015 estimate); Kummu, Taka & Guillaume (2015 GDP PPP, constant 2011 international dollars); HYDE 3.3, 2023 release (reconstructed population from 10,000 BC to 2023); Natural Earth (boundaries).

HYDE 3.3 updates the 2017 HYDE 3.2 methodology. This atlas integrates the 126 epochs in the HYDE 3.3 baseline NetCDF, whose metadata identifies the March 2023 run. Its earlier values are historical reconstructions.

Cells are calculated on a 2,048 × 2,048 population count grid. The displayed transformation is a smoothed optimal-transport map with ocean space retained. Numerical checks · Sources & reuse · Source & research
