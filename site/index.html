Select a cell to follow it · Scroll to zoom

Each cell contains approximately one million people. On ordinary geography, these cells are small in densely inhabited places and large elsewhere. The transformation stretches dense places and contracts sparse places, carrying the same cells, dots and country boundaries together. Some geographical space is retained for recognition. The colour lenses show economic output differences on the same population world.

Each shape represents a patch of land and the people living there. Select one to follow it between the two views.

Some geography is retained to keep the world recognisable, so the shapes and their displayed areas are not perfectly equal. A shape can cross a national border.

Read the colour.

Output per person compares economic output in 2015. The history view compares each place’s share of that output with its share of all the years people have lived, from 10,000 BC to 2023.

Population is a 2025 projection; earlier history is reconstructed. These comparisons describe places, not individual incomes or the causes of wealth.
